The repo's still alive, the domain's renewal date is coming up, and the README still says "Coming soon." You dreamed of side project monetization, but sign-ups are in the double digits and Stripe is silent. Right before you hit delete, there's one more exit worth considering: selling the abandoned app as an MVP prototype.

1. What the community is saying right now

The recurring struggle in the indie/side-project scene boils down to three points.

  • Building has gotten easier (AI, vibe coding) and shipping has gotten faster, but the rate that actually reaches side project monetization is still low. Public write-ups keep landing on more than half of shipped products sitting at $0 revenue.
  • More people are closing repos with server bills, domain costs, and mental debt piled up, stuck on "someday I'll market it," without ever selling the abandoned app first.
  • At the same time, demand for "I'd rather not build this from scratch" and interest in buying an MVP prototype is still very much alive. The problem is that the marketplace, trust, and price discovery for this are scattered.

2. The insight — when handing it off beats letting it sit or deleting it

You don't need to be embarrassed about failing. Most indie makers ship several times and stop several times. The problem isn't the failure itself — it's that abandoning something with no exit keeps levying an opportunity cost.

What hitting delete doesn't solve

  • The late nights, learning, and domain costs you already spent don't get refunded.
  • The code, UI, and idea can still be a "time-saving package" for someone else.
  • Indie hacker income stories are heavily survivorship-biased. Far more projects are quietly asleep somewhere.

When selling the abandoned app is the smart move

  • You can't realistically touch it even once a week anymore.
  • Only upkeep costs go out, and the learning curve is already done.
  • Your focus is more valuable spent on a different idea.

Here, side project monetization doesn't have to mean "$10k MRR." Turning it into coffee money, server costs, or seed for the next sprint is a perfectly reasonable form of monetization too. And that path often starts with selling the MVP prototype.

The limits of resale communities and heavy M&A brokers

  • Used-goods communities: fraud, no escrow, no pricing standard. Source/account transfer disputes are common.
  • Big M&A brokers: prototypes with no revenue or traffic get filtered out at the door.
  • Selling to people you know: price negotiation gets tangled up with the relationship.

What's actually needed isn't a "huge exit" — it's a marketplace where your listing is discoverable by keyword, price is found through public bidding, and there's a staged deal flow.
