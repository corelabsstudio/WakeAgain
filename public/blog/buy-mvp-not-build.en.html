Sometimes buying a sleeping MVP beats building your side project from scratch

2026-07-24 · WakeAgain Growth Team
Keywords: sell side project · abandoned app · sell MVP · indie hacker exit

Indie maker feeds are full of "shipped it in a weekend" stories, but in reality weeks evaporate into auth, payments, permissions, deployment, and admin screens. When you have the idea and access to customers but implementation is the bottleneck, you have two options: open yet another empty repo, or bring in an existing MVP prototype and build on the scaffolding that's already there.

1. What's actually going on — demand exists, but the channel is scattered

  • Questions like "I want to buy code and pivot" or "is there a SaaS skeleton similar to this?" keep showing up in communities. The problem is that trustworthy marketplaces for this are scattered.
  • On the seller side there's a supply of people looking to sell an abandoned app, and on the buyer side there's demand from people who'd rather not build from scratch — both exist at the same time.
  • People chasing side project monetization and indie hacker income are also looking for a path that cuts validation and build time, rather than redoing the entire build.

2. The insight — when buying wins

When buying is the better move

  • You've picked your niche but don't have time for a full-stack build
  • A listing already exists with a similar UI or domain model
  • Your goal is speed to launch or pivot, not the learning itself

When building it yourself is better

  • The core value is in a proprietary algorithm, data, or brand
  • You need full control over the tech stack
  • The listing's description and transfer docs are too thin

When buying, don't just look for "cheap" — look at whether it's discounted relative to rebuild cost. Even with no users or revenue, a well-organized prototype can be an asset that cuts down weeks of work. For a seller, that's exactly the pitch behind selling an MVP prototype.

A checklist before you buy

  • Whether there's a demo, screenshots, and how to run it
  • License, third-party keys, and how much of the domain is included
  • The transfer process and support scope (e.g. a few days of Q&A)
  • Roughly how much work it'll take to adapt it to your niche
